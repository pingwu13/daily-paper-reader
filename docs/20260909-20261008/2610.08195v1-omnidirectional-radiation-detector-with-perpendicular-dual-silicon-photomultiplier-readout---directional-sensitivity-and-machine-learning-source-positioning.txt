# Omnidirectional Radiation Detector with Perpendicular Dual Silicon Photomultiplier Readout - Directional Sensitivity and Machine Learning Source Positioning

Abstract 

Directional information of incident gamma rays in radiation detec-tion is essential to many applications, from medical imaging to mapping radioactive contamination after nuclear accidents, characterization of nu-clear waste during decommissioning, and nuclear security. In this work, we present a radiation detector capable of detecting gamma-ray photons in 4 π, utilizing Gadolinium Aluminum Gallium Garnet (GAGG) scintil-lating crystals and silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs). The GAGG crystals are assembled in a cubical 4 ×4×4 matrix, and their light output is read out by SiPMs from two neighboring sides. The size of each crystal is 3×3×3 mm 3, while the matrix pitch is 3.2 mm, matching the size and the pitch of the 4 ×4 SiPMs for one-to-one coupling. The layers perpendicular to the SiPMs are separated by optical reflectors, providing efficient light collection while lowering the probability of inter-crystal leakage. The de-sign of the radiation detector offers high detection efficiency and full-view imaging of gamma-ray sources through Compton scattering. Monte Carlo simulations were performed in Geant4 to evaluate the detection efficiency of the proposed design due to its geometrical non-uniformity. Machine learning models developed with the XGBoost algorithm were trained and tested on the simulated data to assess the capability of the detector to localize point sources of 511, 662, and 1275 keV energies. We find that the proposed design does not influence the collection efficiency of the high-energy gamma-ray photons and offers sensitivity to the direction of the incoming gamma rays. The trained models are capable of determining the source-to-detector distance of the Na-22 and Cs-137 point sources from measurements, thus providing initial conditions for faster image recon-struction.

Knowledge of directional information is a crucial element in the imaging of gamma-emitting sources, occurring in many applications of radiation detection, from medical imaging and nuclear security to nuclear decommissioning and post-nuclear disaster decontamination of released radionuclides, such as Cs-137 [1, 2]. Common methods of detection include position-sensitive, energy-resolving de-tectors, often limited in their field of view (FOV) and detection sensitivity, requiring heavy collimators or coded aperture approaches. These methods need multiple detectors or additional equipment for rotation to cover the full FOV, creating further complications such as increased weight and price, as well as longer acquisition times. In this work, we present a novel type of directional 4 π

radiation detector, covering the full FOV with depth of interaction (DOI) infor-mation, without additional appliances (Fig. 1). The design relies on detecting the scattered and absorbed gamma rays in one volume of a segmented scintil-lating detector, read out from two sides by silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs). It consists of 64 voxels, each voxel being a Gadolinium Aluminum Gallium Gar-net (GAGG) cube. The SiPMs are perpendicular to each other, sitting on two neighboring faces of the cubical volume. Other similar cubical designs have more complicated readout configurations, for example, the SiPMs coupled to the four lateral faces of the detector [3], or all six of the faces covered by SiPMs [4, 5, 6]. The presented radiation detector has the modality of a Compton Camera (CC), a gamma-ray imaging device that utilizes electronic collimation. Typi-cally, a CC has two layers: a layer in which the incoming gamma ray scatters and transmits part of its energy to the recoil electron in the material, and a second layer in which the gamma ray is photoelectrically absorbed. Each layer in this configuration has its own readout, but this design is still limited to a small FOV and low detection efficiency, as the sensitive component of the CC is only one side of the detector exposed to incoming gamma rays. As each voxel cube of the proposed detector design can act as a scatterer and an absorber, the detection efficiency is enhanced, and the compactness of the detection system is improved. Unlike other proposed parallel dual read-out CCs [7, 8], this design does not require calculating signal ratios for DOI determination. The capability of this radiation detector design to act as a CC has been previously explored, and images of a Cs-137 point source positioned in front of one face and one ver-tex have been reconstructed [9]. To reconstruct an image of a radiation source, reconstruction algorithms, such as simple and forward back-projection, require information of the source position before the reconstruction [10, 11]. Maximum likelihood estimation methods (like Maximum Likelihood Expectation Maxi-mization - MLEM) do not require prior knowledge of the source position, but providing initial values would greatly reduce computing requirements and re-construction time. In this paper, we utilize the eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) machine learning library for determination of the source-to-detector distance from raw measurements. We show that the detector itself is sensitive to the direction of the incoming gamma rays through attenuation in its sensi-tive volume, enabling source positioning prior to the reconstruction. Machine learning (ML) methods have been successfully applied to problems of local-ization and identification of gamma-ray sources [12, 13, 14, 15], as well as for event selection and detection optimization [16, 17]. XGBoost library has been 2Figure 1: Visualisation of the simulated detector setup for the proposed omnidi-rectional radiation detector design. The voxels are GAGG crystals (in yellow), with reflectors (in white) and SiPMs (in cyan). previously utilized for tracking of the interactions in scintillator-SiPM particle detectors [18], but its capability for source-to-detector distance determination is explored here for the first time. We also examine the detection efficiency of the proposed design for gamma-ray energies of Na-22 and Cs-137 point sources. The efficiencies at these energies are relevant for experimental measurements, as two sides of the sensitive volume are covered by SiPMs. 

# 2 Methods 

2.1 Detector design 

We propose a novel type of gamma-ray detector with depth of interaction (DOI) capability, comprised of 64 scintillating crystals, as depicted in Fig. 1. The crystals are arranged in a 4x4x4 voxel array in a cubical formation, with crystal sizes of 3x3x3 mm 3 and a 3.2 mm matrix pitch. The read-out consists of two silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs), positioned perpendicularly to each other on adjacent faces of the segmented detector cube, with 1:1 coupling to the crystals. The SiPMs are 4x4 matrices of pixels, with pixel sizes of 3x3 mm 2 and a 3.2 mm pitch, which correspond to the sizes of the respective voxels to which they are attached. Reflectors, modelled as titanium dioxide (Ti0 2), are placed in planes that are perpendicular to both SiPMs, in between the 4x4 layers of the crystals. The reflectors provide maximal light collection by the SiPMs, as well as lower the possibility of inter-crystal light leakage from one plane to another. Additionally, reflectors encompass the outer faces of the detectors that are not in contact with the SiPMs.

2.4 Machine learning methods 

A machine learning (ML) model was developed with the XGBoost library [23], which utilizes supervised learning algorithms based on gradient-boosted decision trees. It is available as an application programming interface (API) in Python’s 5scikit-learn library [24]. XGBoost library is widely used for ML tasks such as classification and regression, since it is designed to be highly efficient, portable, and flexible. The draft of the code for utilization of the XGBoost library was developed using Claude Opus 4.8. As the point sources of the 511, 662, and 1275 keV energies were positioned around the detector at various distances, there were 924 classes from: • 3 energies (511, 662 and 1275 keV), • 14 positions in front of faces and vertices, • and 22 distances at each position, ranging from 10 mm to 300 mm. The grid spacing of the training distances from 10 mm to 100 mm was 10 mm, from 100 mm to 200 mm it was 12.5 mm, and from 200 mm to 300 mm it was 25 mm. The features chosen for training were: • occupancy of each of the 64 detector voxels, i.e., the fractions of events that fired in each voxel, • photopeak fraction at ±2σ from the peak, • 3 multiplicity fractions: 1-voxel, 2-voxel and (3+)-voxel events, • log-rate of the number of collected events, resulting in 132 features in total. The occupancy of the voxels will vary during irradiation from different source positions, as the gamma rays attenuate in the detector, and its effect can be seen in Figure 3. The energy depositions are known, as it is assumed that the detector is calibrated. The multiplicity fractions slightly differ between faces and vertices, and the number of collected events, represented by the log-rate, will depend on the energy and position of the source, but also on its distance from the detector. The total of 50 million events per class was separated into three categories: 60% for training, 20% for validation, and 20% for testing. After Gaussian smearing of the energy resolution (Eq. 2), an energy cutoff at 100 keV was applied to correspond to the realistic experimental conditions of background noise removal. The pseudomeasurement grouping was defined as 200 000 lines per class. The XGBoost ML model’s regression capabilities were utilized for the deduction of the source’s distance from the face or the vertex in front of which it was positioned. The hyperparameters relevant for the model’s training are summarized in Table 1. The regression was performed on the logarithm of the Distance (log 10 (Distance)) to minimize the errors at large Distances, and transformed back after regression.

# 3 Results 

3.1 Detector’s response due to the geometrical non-uniformity 

The proposed design of the detector relies on the SiPM read-out from two neigh-boring faces of the detector cube. The effect of this geometrical non-uniformity of the proposed detector system was studied by efficiency calculations of the gamma-ray photons that deposited more than 10 keV of their energy in one of the voxels in the detector module. The ϵint , ϵgeom , and ϵabs were measured and calculated for all of the detector’s faces and vertices at source energies corre-sponding to Na-22 and Cs-137 at 511, 662, and 1275 keV, at source-to-detector distances ranging from 10 mm to 300 mm. The results can be seen in Figs. 4 and 5, comparing the efficiencies for the faces and vertices of the detector at the selected energies and distances, respectively. It can be observed that intrinsic efficiency lowers with increasing energy at every distance (Figs. 4a and 4b), for both faces and vertices. The intrinsic efficiency loss from 511 keV to 1275 keV point source energies is from ∼38% for 10 mm source-to-detector distance to 

∼33% for 300 mm separation if the source is in front of the faces, while for the vertices it is approximately 37% at every distance. The intrinsic efficiencies for 511, 662 and 1275 keV energies for faces (Fig. 5a) and vertices (Fig. 5b) at dif-ferent source-to-detector distances show opposite tendencies. The faces exhibit an increase in intrinsic efficiencies as the distance increase from 10 mm to 300 mm: ∼50% for 511 keV, ∼60% for 662 keV, and ∼65% for 1275 keV. Intrinsic efficiency values for the vertices in the same distance interval are lower by ∼11% for 511 keV, ∼12% for 662 keV, and ∼13% for 1275 keV point source energies. Geometric efficiencies do not depend on the source energies, but change expo-nentially with the source-to-detector distance. The absolute efficiencies for point sources of different energies also exponentially decrease with distance, but for distances below 100 mm, the energies show slight differentiation, as the lowest energy of 511 keV has the highest absolute efficiency of up to 2% for the closest positions, while the 662 keV and 1275 keV energies have lower efficiencies of 1.7% and 1.25%, respectively.

Geant4 simulations were conducted for exploration of the detection sensitivity of a novel radiation detector, capable of determining the position of the source in 4 π. As its readout is positioned on two sensitive volume faces, it raised concerns about the detector’s capability for effective collection of gamma rays from all sides. The results have shown that the influence of the geometrical non-uniformity of the design imposes no restrictions on the detection sensitivity from all sides of the detector, as the differences in the efficiencies between the differ-ent faces and vertices are less than 1%. The intrinsic efficiency will suffer from edge effects when measured by a point source. This effect can be seen in Figure 5, when the source is positioned in front of the faces, as the intrinsic efficiency of the vertices is higher than for faces at the lowest distance at respective energies. The intrinsic efficiency of the faces increases with the distance of the source, as expected for the larger cross-section, while it decreases for the vertices. The geometrical efficiency falls with increasing distance as well, at the same rate for all energies and all source positions. The highest absolute efficiency is achieved for the lowest source-to-detector distance of 10 mm at 511 keV, but the differ-ences between its values at different energies fade with increasing distance from 100 mm to 300 mm. Due to the detector’s small size, with 2 cm 3 of sensitive volume, the absolute efficiency of the detector setup converges to <0.1% for all three tested energies after 100 mm distance. The localization of the source prior to the image reconstruction was also one of the goals of the study. The ML regression models were built with the XGBoost library, utilizing the Geant4 simulated data for training, validation, and testing. The results indicate that this algorithm can determine the source-to-detector distance with accuracy higher than half of the training grid spacing for 511, 662, and 1275 keV point source energies, without overfitting and with stable convergence of the models. To test the validity of the predictions of the models, the three distances at 60, 125, and 200 mm were held out during training. Interpolation of these distances has shown that the prediction offset from their true value is less than 3% in the least accurate model for 1275 keV and 125 mm interpolation distance, while the other models had better predictions. The interpolation results demonstrate that the regression models correctly capture continuous distance dependence, and that the XGBoost algorithm is applicable to the problem of source-to-detector distance determination. As the models’ interpolation RMSE remains under 7% for 200 mm for the 1275 keV source energy, it can be concluded that this approach could be expanded with the training grid of sources set to distances larger than 300 mm. However, the exact constraints of the method require further investigation. The light-sharing principle of the detector can be extended to larger setups, either by increasing the size of the voxels or their number. The concept of source positioning with the XGBoost algorithm could then be applied for training ML models for sources even further away from the detector. The detection efficiency and modeling errors could be influenced by the lim-itations of the simulation setup. The voxels in the detector matrix could have different experimental energy resolutions, depending on their positions. Due to the bright nature of GAGG crystals, with light output of 54 000 photons/MeV, high self-transparency, and compactness of the detector’s body, these differences should be small. The experimental construction of the detector would also in-15 clude plastic black encapsulation for noise suppression, which could contribute to scatter detection. Due to the detector’s overall small size and low weight, it would allow for easy portability, as it could be hand-held, mounted on a drone, or on a robotic arm. The absolute position of the source could be determined with a denser grid of point sources surrounding the detector, but the informa-tion about the general direction of the gamma ray source and its distance from the detector’s face or vertex is enough to speed up image reconstruction by providing initial spatial parameters. 

# 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we presented the design of a novel omnidirectional segmented radi-ation detector, utilizing dual perpendicular SiPM readout. Its performance was tested with Monte Carlo simulations in Geant4. We found that detection effi-ciency for energies relevant to experimental measurements of Na-22 and Cs-137 point sources is not affected by its geometrical non-uniformity. The XGBoost machine learning algorithm successfully predicted the separation distance from the point sources to the detector’s faces and vertices. This demonstrates the detector’s adequacy for omnidirectional radiation detection, combined with the detector’s capability to determine the position of the source through attenuation of the gamma rays and occupancy of the crystals they interact with. The de-termination of the source’s position from raw measurements of the gamma rays could significantly reduce image reconstruction duration and computational re-quirements when the detector is applied as a Compton camera. 16 Acknowledgments
